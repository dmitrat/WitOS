# S8.3 — A collector's decommits in place

Plan step S8.3, found while measuring R9.2 on VMware (2026-10-28): a crossgen2 in the guest, compiling PowerShell's
assemblies with the framework and PowerShell as references, made the namespace service serve the mappings of some 300
assemblies chunk by chunk, and the service died after a minute with `SIGSEGV`, which took every volume with it.

## 1. What was measured

- The service's report, named through its `.dbg`: `memset` in the dynamic linker, called by the collector's
  `gc_heap::a_fit_segment_end_p` ← `uoh_try_fit` ← `allocate_uoh` ← `GCHeap::Alloc` ← `RhpNewVariableSizeObject` ←
  `NamespaceSession.MapAsync`, which allocates a 256 KiB array for each chunk it maps (R4.2). The fault was a write to
  a page that is not present (error 6) in the collector's range: the collector cleared memory it believed committed.
  H5.3 had seen the same fault under a cache experiment with 512-byte blocks and left it unexplained.
- CoreCLR's collector on Unix commits with `mprotect(PROT_READ | PROT_WRITE)` and decommits with an anonymous
  `mmap(MAP_FIXED, PROT_NONE)` over its reserved range (`gcenv.unix.cpp`'s `VirtualCommit` and `VirtualDecommit`).
  A failed decommit is not handled ("the GC does not handle the failure gracefully"): the collector goes on taking the
  range for committed.
- The libc served an anonymous `MAP_FIXED` as every `MAP_FIXED`: the range released from its reservation, which the
  kernel shrinks or splits in two around a middle part (S5.1), then reserved again at its address. A decommit inside a
  range therefore split its reservation in three, two more kernel reservations and two more entries of the library's
  table each time, and nothing merged them back, where Linux merges an anonymous replacement into its neighbours. A
  process whose collector decommits for a long time spends the 8192 reservations of its table (N2.2); then the
  reservation of a range fails after the range was released, `mmap` answers `ENOMEM`, and the collector writes into
  pages that no longer exist.
- The spawn scenario's dynamic program measures it (below): 32 decommits of every other page of a 64-page mapping
  left the kernel's reservation count higher before the change and unchanged after it.
- The same R9.2 measurement on a disk built with the change ran through: crossgen2 compiled the 59 assemblies in
  224 s while the namespace service served every mapping, and `exit` ended the session with the services stopped
  and the volumes clean. Without it the service died 68 s into the measurement, among the first and largest
  assemblies, each of whose compilations maps every reference; every later request of the session's processes to
  `/data` then failed with `EINVAL`, crossgen2's start among them.

## 2. The change

- `src/Substrate/libc/memory.c`: an anonymous `MAP_FIXED` whose range the library's plain mappings cover whole is a
  replacement in place (`replace_plain`): `MEMORY_DECOMMIT` of the range in each reservation it lies in, then, unless
  the protection is `PROT_NONE`, `MEMORY_COMMIT` under the protection, which gives fresh zero pages, as Linux's
  replacement does. The reservations and the library's entries stay as they are, so a collector's decommits cost the
  table nothing. Every other `MAP_FIXED` keeps the release and the reservation. An unmapping that splits a plain
  mapping is refused with `ENOMEM` before the kernel splits it when the library's table is full, so that the kernel
  never holds a reservation the library lost.
- `tests/User/dynamic_main.c`: the dynamic program of the spawn scenario commits a 64-page reservation made with
  `PROT_NONE`, decommits every other page with an anonymous `MAP_FIXED` of `PROT_NONE`, and requires the kernel's
  reservation count unchanged (`MEMORY_QUERY`), the recommitted pages zero and the others kept, a writable
  replacement in place zero, and the release to give back one reservation. Before the change the scenario failed on
  x64 with `decommits inside a mapping keep its reservation`; after it, it passes.
