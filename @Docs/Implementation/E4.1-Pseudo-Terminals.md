# E4.1 — Pseudo-terminals

Plan step E4.1 (phase E, goal Ц6). Step E4 puts Arlecchino.Commander to work as the file manager of the developer
scenario. In this scenario a program written on Windows is copied in and run from the file manager. Commander runs a
typed command inside a pseudo-terminal of its own making and shows what the command prints. A program that claims the
screen gets the real terminal through Commander for as long as it holds it (`Passing.Between`). Commander knows only
Linux, macOS and Windows: on any other system it runs commands on pipes. There, no command has a terminal, and an
interactive program does not work under the panels (C4.2 §2). WitOS had no pseudo-terminals. This step adds them.

RFC 0020 §27 asks for pseudo-terminal semantics where they are needed. It lets WitOS map them to PTY-compatible behavior
without inheriting the Unix implementation. Its open question 2 (§58) asks whether that means a PTY-compatible API, a
session API, or both. For the substrate this step answers both:
- the libc gives the Linux calls that programs reach through P/Invoke (`/dev/ptmx`, `/dev/pts/<number>`, musl's
  `posix_openpt`, `grantpt`, `unlockpt` and `ptsname`, unchanged);
- below them, the terminal service gives a pseudo-terminal as a session of its protocol.

Nothing here is an application API. Applications see System.Console, and a terminal emulator sees the Linux calls.

## 1. The terminal service

A terminal of the service is a `TerminalHub`, which holds four things:
- the line discipline;
- the size;
- the sessions (`ITerminalSession`);
- the links to its devices (`ITerminalLink`).

A link has two operations: `Write`, a program's output, which may wait for the device, and `Echo`, the discipline's
echo, which never waits. The console's hub has the consoles' `ConsoleLink`s. A pseudo-terminal (`PseudoTerminal`) is a
hub of its own, the slave side, whose one link is the pseudo-terminal itself. The master side is a session:
- what the master writes is typed at the slave's discipline (`Type`);
- what the slave's programs write, after output processing, queues for the master to read, together with the echo.

Linux's pty behaviors carried over:
- **Output queue.** A program's write waits while 64 KiB wait unread, as Linux's buffer of a pty makes a writer wait.
  The echo never waits, since the master's own write produces it; beyond 256 KiB it is dropped.
- **Hangup to the master.** Once a slave session joined and the last one left, the master's read of nothing answers
  `EIO` and its poll says `HANGUP`, as Linux's master does. Until a slave joined, the master's reads wait. A WitOS child
  connects to the service after `posix_spawn` returned, so an early `EIO` would end a terminal emulator's reader before
  its child spoke.
- **Hangup to the slave.** Once the master went, the slave hangs up. Its sessions get `EVENT_HANGUP` (SIGHUP), a read of
  nothing answers the end of the file, a write answers `EIO`, and no session joins.
- **Size.** The master sets the size; the slave's sessions get `EVENT_SIZE` (SIGWINCH) when it changes.
- **Signal characters.** They reach the slave's sessions as on the console.
- **Numbers.** `PseudoTerminals` gives the lowest free number below 16 (`WIT_TERMINAL_PSEUDO_MAXIMUM`). A number goes back
  once the master and the last slave session went, as devpts reuses its numbers.

The protocol (`witos/terminal.h`, `TerminalMessages`) stays version 1:

| Element | Effect |
|---|---|
| `OPEN_PSEUDO` (11) | On the clients' endpoint, with CONNECT's handles and the events' end required: a new pseudo-terminal, whose master the session is. |
| `CONNECT` with the flag `PSEUDO` and `Offset` the number | Joins the slave side of that pseudo-terminal. A refused request closes the session's end, which the client's first request meets. |
| `GET_NUMBER` (12) | The pseudo-terminal's number; `ENOTTY` on the console. |
| `SET_SIZE` (13) | The columns in the low 16 bits of `Flags`, the rows in the high ones; `ENOTTY` on the console, whose size its device gives. |
| `POLL`'s `HANGUP` (4) | The other side went. |

A master session serves `READ` (its queue), `WRITE` (typing), the attributes and the size of the slave's discipline
(tcgetattr through the master, as Linux allows), `POLL`, and `FLUSH`. `FLUSH`'s `OUTPUT` drops the master's queue and
its `INPUT` drops the slave's typed input. A slave session serves what a console session serves, plus `GET_NUMBER` and
`SET_SIZE`.

The stop stops the console's sessions, every pseudo-terminal's slave sessions and its master. The stop line names the
pseudo-terminals still open, and stays as it was when there are none.

`PseudoTerminalTests` test the service on the host:
- a line typed at the master with its echo through ONLCR;
- the slave's output through output processing;
- no hangup before a slave joined;
- the master's `EIO` after the last slave left, and a slave joining again;
- the master's close hanging up the slave;
- a resize told once;
- Ctrl-C as `EVENT_INTERRUPT`;
- a writer waiting for room while the echo does not;
- the numbers.

## 2. The libc

**The process's terminal.** It is the console, or the slave side of a pseudo-terminal when its creator started it there.
A `WitStreamMessage` of the new end `WIT_STREAM_TERMINAL` (`witos/start.h`) names that pseudo-terminal in its `Terminal`
field and carries no handles. `start.c` takes it before `main` (`__wit_terminal_assign`). The process's two sessions
then CONNECT with `PSEUDO` and that number. The standard streams, termios, `TIOCGWINSZ`, `poll`, the events thread and
its signals work as they do on the console, since this terminal is the process's controlling terminal. A stream
message's `Reserved` became `Terminal`; a pipe's stream message still requires zero there.

**Descriptors.** `/dev/ptmx` and `/dev/pts/<number>` are descriptors of a new kind, `KindPseudo` (`files.c`), over a
record of `terminal.c`:
- **Lifetime.** Its descriptors and the calls in flight reference it, and the last reference closes its session. The
  service then meets the session's end as the master's end or as a slave session's end.
- **Opening.** The open happens outside the table lock (`syscall.c`): `OPEN_PSEUDO`, or CONNECT of the slave side, then
  `GET_NUMBER`, which a refused session fails. The answers are `EAGAIN` when all 16 are taken and `ENOENT` for a slave
  of no pseudo-terminal, as devpts answers.
- **Reading.** A record has one session with an events channel and no thread. A read asks with `NONBLOCK`. With nothing
  there, it takes the events off the channel, asks again, and waits on the channel for a slice of 10 ms. So no wakeup is
  lost, and a descriptor's close never has to interrupt a reader that waits on its handle.
- **poll.** `poll` asks the service outside the table lock, as it asks the network service about a socket. A parked poll
  looks every 10 ms while a record lives (`__wit_poll_sources`).
- **ioctl.**
  - `TIOCGPTN` (ptsname) and `TIOCSPTLCK` (unlockpt) on a master; a slave opens without the unlock.
  - `TIOCSWINSZ`, `TIOCGWINSZ`, `TCGETS`, `TCSETS`, `TCSETSW`, `TCSETSF`, `TCFLSH` and `FIONREAD` reach the
    service.
  - `FIONBIO` sets the description's `O_NONBLOCK`.
  - `TIOCSCTTY` and `TIOCNOTTY` succeed and do nothing: there is no job control.
- **fstat.** A character device: `/dev/ptmx` is 5, 2; a slave is 136, *n*, devpts's numbers.
- **An opened slave** is no controlling terminal: its signal events are taken and dropped.

**posix_spawn** gives a child a slave as its standard streams, as a terminal emulator does:
- **`addopen`.** `posix_spawn_file_actions_addopen` of `/dev/pts/<number>` at a standard descriptor puts the slave there.
  It was `ENOSYS`, the "file as a stream" refusal, and still is for any other path.
- **`adddup2`.** `adddup2` from a standard descriptor that is a terminal stream, or from a slave's descriptor, does the
  same.
- **Inheritance.** A child inherits its creator's pseudo-terminal at every standard stream that is its creator's
  terminal. A program started by a shell that runs under a terminal emulator is on the same pseudo-terminal: pwsh under
  Commander, then `dotnet App.dll` under pwsh.
- **One terminal per child.** A child's terminal streams name one pseudo-terminal, and none of its streams may be the
  console's terminal beside them: both are `ENOSYS`, since a process has one terminal.
- **Sessions.** `POSIX_SPAWN_SETSID` stays `ENOSYS`. Commander asks for it first and starts the child without it when it
  is refused, as it does on a system without sessions.

## 3. The gate

The `runtime-terminal` scenario's `/bin/init` (`tests/User/terminal_init.c`) ends its own checks, then starts
`/bin/pty-check` (`tests/User/pty_check.c`) and requires its exit with 0. Nothing is typed for it.

The check first does what a terminal emulator does, the way Arlecchino.Commander's POSIX terminal does it:
1. `posix_openpt`, `grantpt`, `unlockpt`, `ptsname`; the master is a character device and a terminal; a size set on it.
2. `posix_spawn` of itself as a child, with `addopen` of the slave at 0 and `adddup2` to 1 and 2. `POSIX_SPAWN_SETSID`
   is tried first.
3. The child sees a terminal of the master's size on its three streams, and its line arrives through ONLCR.
4. A line typed at the master reaches the child's canonical read after its echo.
5. With the echo turned off through the master (`tcsetattr`), the next line arrives without one.
6. A new size reaches the child as SIGWINCH.
7. A grandchild the child starts inherits the pseudo-terminal and sees its size.
8. Ctrl-C typed at the master reaches the child as SIGINT, and the child exits with 42.
9. The master then reads `EIO`, and `poll` says `POLLHUP`.

Then it checks openpty's way within one process:
1. The slave opened by its name is a terminal, and the size reaches it.
2. Bytes go both ways.
3. The master reads `EIO` once the slave closed.
4. `/dev/pts` of no pseudo-terminal is `ENOENT`.

The scenario requires three lines:
- `[PTY] emulator: the child ran on /dev/pts/`;
- `[PTY] pseudo-terminals on <isa>: `;
- ` checks passed, 0 failed`.

The terminal service's stop line is now required without its count of sessions. A process that just ended leaves
sessions for the service to see go, and a stop may come first: with `/bin/init` waiting one more second after the check
the line said 0, and without the wait it said 1. The required text still ends `sessions stopped, 1 console released`,
which no pseudo-terminal left open would allow.

## 5. Limits kept explicit

- **epoll.** A pseudo-terminal's descriptor in `epoll_ctl` is `EPERM`, as a file is. `poll`, `ppoll` and blocking and
  non-blocking reads serve it; a .NET program's console on a slave is its standard streams, which do not change.
- **Job control.** There are no process groups, sessions or foreground groups. A signal character reaches every process
  whose controlling terminal the slave is, as on the console.
- **Fixed sizes.** 16 pseudo-terminals at once, and 8 pseudo-terminal descriptors' records in a process (masters and
  opened slaves).
- **Paths and inheritance.** No `/dev/tty`, no `stat` of `/dev/pts` paths, no packet mode, and no pseudo-terminal
  descriptor inherited beyond the standard streams. A child takes no descriptor but its standard ones (S6.2).
- **Authority.** A slave joins by its number. Like the console, the service hands its terminals to whoever holds its
  clients' endpoint, which the process manager gives every process.
