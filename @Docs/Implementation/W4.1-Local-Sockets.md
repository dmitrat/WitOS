# W4.1 — local sockets

Plan step W4.1, the first slice of W4 in `W0-Network-Inventory.md` (decision 8: local sockets are the libc's own,
within and between processes as shared pipes are, and need no network service). Processes talk over `AF_UNIX`
sockets as on Linux: `socketpair`, a listening socket bound to a path of a mounted volume or to an abstract name, and
connections between processes. Upstream .NET, built unchanged, reaches them through `UnixDomainSocketEndPoint`,
System.IO.Pipes (a named pipe is a Unix domain socket in the temporary directory on Unix) and the runtime's diagnostic
IPC, which dotnet-counters and dotnet-trace speak.

| .NET over them | nothing of WitOS's | the Ipc area of the N1 suite in `runtime-compatibility` |

## 1. The names

A socket's name is the namespace service's (`src/Services/Namespace/Server/SocketNames.cs`), since the service already
resolves every path below a mount point for every process, and a name is how one process finds another's socket.

- **A path** below a mount point: `SOCKET_BIND` refuses a name a volume's entry or another socket has (`EADDRINUSE`), a
  missing directory (`ENOENT`), a directory that is a file (`ENOTDIR`) and a read-only volume (`EROFS`). The name then
  stands beside the volume's entries: `STAT` reports `WIT_NAMESPACE_KIND_SOCKET` with the mode 0755, a listing of its
  directory holds it, `REMOVE` unlinks it, `RENAME` moves it within its volume (and a renamed directory moves the names
  below it), `OPEN` answers `ENXIO` as Linux's open of a socket does, `MKDIR` over it `EEXIST`, `rmdir` of a directory
  that holds one `ENOTEMPTY`. As on Linux, a path stays a socket's name after its socket closes, until it is unlinked;
  a connection to it is then `ECONNREFUSED`.
- **An abstract name** (`WIT_NAMESPACE_ABSTRACT`), Linux's, of up to 107 bytes: in no directory, and gone with its
  socket or its session; a connection to an abstract name nobody has is `ECONNREFUSED`, as on Linux.
- **The listener's end.** A bound socket that listens moves the service the sending end of a channel of its own
  (`SOCKET_LISTEN`, `SEND`, `DUPLICATE` and `TRANSFER`). `SOCKET_CONNECT` answers with a duplicate of it (`SEND` and
  `TRANSFER`), or `ENOENT` for no name, `ECONNREFUSED` for a name nobody listens on or a file that is no socket, and
  `EPROTOTYPE` for a socket of another type. The service keeps the end until the socket closes (`SOCKET_CLOSE`), the
  name is unlinked or the binding's session ends, which also takes its abstract names away.
- **No byte of a connection passes through the service.** A request's first handle reaches the session through the
  connection (`NamespaceConnection`), which closes whatever the session leaves.

The names live in the service's memory: a path is not an inode of the volume, and the names are gone at the next boot,
where Linux keeps a socket's inode on the disk.

## 2. The libc's local sockets

`src/Substrate/libc/unix.c` serves `AF_UNIX` before `socket.c` sees a call (`syscall.c` asks `__wit_local_call`,
`__wit_local_transfer` and `__wit_local_ioctl` first; a descriptor of `files.c`'s `KindLocal` names a record).

- **Types.** `socket` serves `SOCK_STREAM` and `SOCK_SEQPACKET`; `socketpair` serves those and `SOCK_DGRAM`. A
  datagram socket of its own has no name to send to and is `ESOCKTNOSUPPORT`, which .NET's
  `Socket.OSSupportsUnixDomainSockets` takes for support, since it asks with a datagram socket and looks for
  `EAFNOSUPPORT` alone.
- **A connection** is two rings of 64 KiB in one region of 33 pages behind a header page: ring *r* carries side *r*'s
  bytes, its tail the writer's word and its head the reader's, free-running counts written with release and read with
  acquire. A stream's ring holds bytes; a sequenced packet's or a datagram's holds records, a length then the bytes, each
  written whole (`EMSGSIZE` beyond a ring) and cut to the reader's room (`MSG_TRUNC`).
- **socketpair** puts the region in a memory object of the process alone; a waiter parks on the header's sequence word,
  which every change bumps.
- **A named connection** may cross processes. `connect` asks the service for the listener's end, makes the region (a
  memory object) and a wakeup channel, and sends the listener a connection message with a duplicate of the object and
  the channel's second end; it completes once the message is queued, as Linux's completes in the listener's backlog,
  and the client may write up to a ring before `accept`. The listener's queue is its channel's, four connections: a
  blocking `connect` that finds it full waits for room, a nonblocking one answers `EAGAIN`, as Linux's does. `accept`
  takes a message, maps the region, checks its header and returns a descriptor; waiting consumes nothing, so every
  thread that waits in `accept` sees each connection the queue holds.
- **Wakeups across processes.** A side sends an empty message on its end after each change of its rings or its state; a
  waiter drains its end, looks at the rings and waits on the end (`OBJECT_WAIT`). Another thread of the process may have
  drained the wakeup a waiter needed, so the wait ends every 10 ms at most; `poll` and `epoll` look as often while such a
  socket lives (`__wit_poll_sources`, as for a shared pipe). The kernel's `PEER_CLOSED` on a side's end says the other
  side's process let its end go: it closed, or it ended.
- **Linux's endings.** A read returns zero once the peer shut its writing or closed, `SHUT_RD` ends the reads; a write
  after `SHUT_WR`, the peer's `SHUT_RD` or its close is `EPIPE` and raises `SIGPIPE` unless `MSG_NOSIGNAL`. A side that
  closes with bytes unread leaves its peer `ECONNRESET` once, and so does a listener that closes with connections queued,
  as Linux's `unix_release_sock` does.
- **poll and epoll** report a socket as Linux's `unix_poll` does: readable with bytes or once the receiving half shut,
  writable with a quarter of a ring free, `POLLHUP` once both halves shut, `POLLRDHUP`, `POLLERR` for a pending reset; an
  unconnected stream socket `POLLOUT` and `POLLHUP`; a listener readable while a connection is queued, which a receive
  with no room tells without taking it. An epoll registration holds no reference, as a network socket's (W2.1b): it is
  known by its record and the record's generation, and its edge token moves with what the peer wrote and read, the
  shutdowns and closes, and a listener's connections.
- **Names.** `bind` takes a path, normalized from the current directory as every path is, below a mount point of the
  namespace service; a path in the package is `EROFS` where its directory exists. `bind` of an address of the family
  alone gives an abstract name of five hexadecimal digits, as Linux's autobind. `getsockname` and `getpeername` give
  the names as bound (a relative path stays relative); an accepted socket's name is the listener's, and its peer's the
  client's own or the family alone.
- **Options.** `SO_TYPE`, `SO_DOMAIN`, `SO_PROTOCOL`, `SO_ACCEPTCONN`, `SO_ERROR`, `SO_SNDBUF` and `SO_RCVBUF` (the ring),
  `SO_RCVTIMEO`, `SO_SNDTIMEO`, `SO_LINGER` and `SO_PEERCRED`, which reports the one account every process runs as and
  the process id every process has; setting the buffer sizes and the generic flags is accepted. No ancillary data is
  served: `sendmsg` with control data is `EOPNOTSUPP`, and `recvmsg` returns none.
- **Records.** 64 sockets a process; a record lives while a descriptor or a call in flight references it, and a bound
  socket's close goes to the service after its last reference, outside the table lock (`__wit_local_flush`).

## 4. Left for later

- Named datagram sockets (`sendto` a path), which nothing of .NET's or the system layer's uses yet.
- Ancillary data: descriptors (`SCM_RIGHTS`) and credentials (`SCM_CREDENTIALS`).
- Every process's id is 1 (`WIT_LIBC_PROCESS_ID`), so the runtime's default diagnostic port,
  `dotnet-diagnostic-<pid>-<key>-socket` in the temporary directory, has one name: the first .NET process takes it and
  the others run without one. A tool reaches any process through a port named for it (`DOTNET_DiagnosticPorts`), as the
  Ipc area's check does; process ids unique across the system are a step of their own.
