# D3.2 — The block protocol over the driver's channel

D3.2 is the second half of D3. It puts the block storage contract of D3.1 on the virtio block driver's channel in the
guest. A client reaches a disk through `IBlockStorage`, and the driver serves reads, writes and flushes through a
window of the client's own memory, which the device reads and writes in place. The `runtime-devices` scenario boots
with a second, writable disk that carries a GUID partition table. Through the protocol, the device manager reads the
table and writes the test partition. After the boot, the tool reads the disk's image and requires the bytes the guest
wrote.

## The protocol (`witos/block.h`)

When a block driver is ready, its answer to the device manager carries one handle: the other end of a block channel
(`witos/driver.h`). On that channel a client sends a `WitBlockRequest` and the driver answers each with a
`WitBlockReply`. Each structure is 40 bytes and starts with `Version` and `Size`. The driver serves one request at a
time.

| Operation | Request | Reply |
|---|---|---|
| `ATTACH` | a duplicate of a memory object in the one handle slot (`MAP`, `WRITE`, `TRANSFER`), `Length` its bytes, whole pages up to 64 | the window's bytes |
| `INFO` | nothing | the device's bytes, its logical block, `READ_ONLY` and `FLUSHES`, the transfer limit |
| `READ` | bytes of the device at `Offset` into the window at `Window` | — |
| `WRITE` | bytes of the window to the device; `DURABLE` makes this write and every completed write durable when it completes | — |
| `FLUSH` | nothing | — |

- **The window.**
  - The client creates the window, maps it and attaches a duplicate of it.
  - The driver pins the window for the device's DMA and closes the handle: the pin holds the object. A later `ATTACH`
    replaces the window.
  - A virtio descriptor then points into the client's pages, so nothing is copied between the device and the window.
    The client copies between the window and its buffers.
- **The checks.** `BlockMessages.Check` is the one check of a request, shared by the driver and the host tests. It
  runs before anything reaches the device:
  - offsets, lengths and window offsets are multiples of the device's block;
  - a length is positive, at most the transfer limit, and lies within the device and the window;
  - `INFO` and `FLUSH` carry nothing.
- **Errors** are errno values:
  - `EINVAL` for a request that cannot be served as it is;
  - `EROFS` for a write to a read-only device;
  - `EOPNOTSUPP` for an unknown operation;
  - `EPROTO`, with operation 0, for bytes that are no request of this version;
  - `EIO` for a request the device failed.
- **The transfer limit.** A transfer touches at most one page more than its whole pages. So the device's segments
  less one bound the limit in pages, within the largest window. The device's segments are what `SEG_MAX` reports,
  within the queue less the header and the status. On QEMU the limit is the whole window, 256 KiB.

## The driver

`src/Services/Drivers/VirtioBlock`:

- **Features.** `VirtioBlockQueue` negotiates `VIRTIO_F_VERSION_1` together with whatever the device offers of
  `SIZE_MAX`, `SEG_MAX`, `RO`, `BLK_SIZE` and `FLUSH`.
- **Configuration.** It reads the device configuration from the BAR's fourth capability. It rereads until the
  configuration generation holds still, and reads a 64-bit field as two 32-bit halves, as the transport requires.
  - The capacity is in 512-byte sectors.
  - The logical block is `BLK_SIZE`'s, otherwise 512 bytes.
  - The segment limits are `SEG_MAX`'s and `SIZE_MAX`'s.
- **The queue** holds up to 128 entries. Its five pages are now one pin with its physical ranges
  (`DmaPin.RunAt`), where D2 had five pins.
- **A request** is a header, the data's segments and a status. The segments are the pin's physical runs, cut at
  `SIZE_MAX`.
  - `IN` marks the data segments device-writable.
  - `OUT` leaves them device-readable.
  - `FLUSH` carries no segments.
- **`BlockServer`** checks each request and serves it. A durable write is `OUT` followed by `FLUSH`, as is `FLUSH`
  itself. On a device without `FLUSH` both complete at once: without a volatile cache, a completed write is already
  durable.
- **The loop.** The driver waits on both channels with one `OBJECT_WAIT`:
  - the device manager's channel, which no message serves yet;
  - the block channel, while its client keeps it.

  When the client closes the block channel, the pin ends. When the device manager closes its own, the driver ends.
- **Its lines.** The driver still reads sector 0 with its own page and reports what is there: the boot disk's FAT
  boot sector, or the data disk's protective MBR. A second line gives the device's bytes, block, flags and transfer
  limit.

## The client (`OutWit.OS.Storage`)

- **`IBlockSession`** is a call and a window.
- **`ChannelBlockSession`** is the session over a block channel. It creates the window, maps it and attaches it.
- **`ChannelBlockStorage`** is the storage contract over a session.
  - It describes the device as the `INFO` reply does. `OptimalTransferSize` is the driver's limit within the window.
  - It sets no limit of its own (`MaxTransferSize` 0). A request goes through the window in transfers of that size.
  - A durable write marks its last transfer `DURABLE`, which the protocol defines to cover the transfers before it.
  - A driver's error is an `IOException`.
  - It serves one request at a time, as its session does. A request blocks its thread on the channel.
- **`GptPartitionTable.WriteAsync`** writes a table (UEFI 2.10, §5.2.3 and §5.3) and reads it back:
  - a protective MBR;
  - the primary header and 128 entries;
  - the backup entries and header;
  - a flush.

  It refuses a partition outside the usable blocks, one that overlaps another, a repeated or out-of-range entry index,
  an empty type and a name longer than 36 characters.
- **`DmaPin.RunAt`** gives the physical address of a byte of a pinned window and the contiguous bytes from it.

## The scenario

- **The data disk.** The tool writes `data-disk.img` anew for every run of `runtime-devices`:
  - 8 MiB with OutWit.OS's own GPT writer;
  - one partition, "WitOS test", from block 2048 to 1 MiB before the end;
  - fixed GUIDs, so every run writes the same image.

  QEMU gives the image to the board as a second, writable `virtio-blk-pci` after the entropy source
  (`BootRequest.DataDisk`).
- **The device manager's probe.** It attaches each ready block driver's channel and prints the device as
  `ChannelBlockStorage` describes it.
  - On the read-only boot disk, it sends a write past the resource's own check, and the driver refuses it with
    `EROFS`. Reading the GPT finds no table there.
  - On the data disk, it reads the table, then writes 320 KiB durably at the start of the test partition, which takes
    two transfers, and reads it back.

  The device manager keeps the disks open for its life, until the namespace service of D5 holds the block channels.
- **The host check.** After the boot, `RuntimeDevices.CheckDataDiskAsync` requires the probe's pattern at the
  partition's start in the image. This shows the write reached the medium, not a cache of the guest's.
- **The required lines.**
  - Both drivers' sector 0 lines and device lines, and `serving the block protocol`.
  - The probe's lines for both disks.
  - `2 drivers ready`.

## Tests

- **`tests/OutWit.OS.Tests`**, 115 tests:
  - the messages' layout, which is the C structures', with round trips, refusals and the check's cases;
  - `ChannelBlockStorage` over `LoopbackBlockSession`, a driver in the test's own process that serves each request
    through the protocol's bytes and the shared check: transfers cut at the limit and at the window, the durable flag
    on the last transfer, a driver's error, an inconsistent driver refused, the session's end;
  - the conformance suite of D3.1, which runs on that provider too, with transfers smaller than the requests;
  - the GPT writer: its table equals the tests' independent image after the protective MBR, its backup serves with
    4096-byte blocks, and the partitions it refuses leave the disk untouched.
- **`tests/WitOS.Dev.Tests`**: the data disk carries the test partition at the blocks the tool names, and the check
  after the boot requires the pattern.

## Results

`runtime-services` passes on both ISAs, and the tool's check finds the 327,680 bytes in the image after each boot.

| | x64 (q35) | ARM64 (virt) |
|---|---|---|
| Boot disk | `pci/00:02.0`: 33,554,432 bytes, read-only, FAT16 boot sector | `pci/00:01.0`: the same |
| Data disk | `pci/00:04.0`: 8,388,608 bytes, writable, protective MBR | `pci/00:03.0`: the same |

- **The devices.** Both disks report blocks of 512 bytes and a volatile cache the driver flushes. Their transfers go
  up to 262,144 bytes in 126 segments: QEMU's `SEG_MAX` of 254, within a queue of 128 less the header and the status.
- **The probe.**
  - The boot disk refuses a write with errno 30 and has no GUID partition table.
  - The data disk's table holds "WitOS test" on blocks 2048–14335. The probe wrote 327,680 bytes durably and read
    them back.
- **Summary.** `2 drivers ready`, then `/bin/init` after the device manager answered.

## Limits kept explicit

- **One request at a time** on the channel and on the queue: no queue depth, no indirect descriptors, no `EVENT_IDX`.
  The storage document's outstanding requests (§11–13) wait for a driver that serves several.
- **Discard and write-zeroes are not negotiated.** `ChannelBlockStorage` reports `SupportsDiscard` false.
- **Durable writes are a write and then a flush**, not native FUA, which virtio-blk lacks. `SupportsForceUnitAccess`
  is false.
- **One client per channel.** The block channel is the device manager's until D5's namespace service. A second
  `ATTACH` replaces the window rather than opening a second session.
- **No IOMMU**, as in D2. The driver programs the device with the physical addresses of a window the client chose.
  The holder of the device is the trust boundary.
- **One INTx line per device.** Two devices that share a line would need the binding the first one holds. On both
  boards the scenario's three PCI functions take different lines.
