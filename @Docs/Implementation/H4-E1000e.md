# H4 — The PC's network: an e1000e driver

H3 gave the PC its disks. H4 gives it its network adapter. VMware gives a machine an Intel 82574L, its "e1000e", beside the
older e1000 and its own vmxnet3; QEMU's `e1000e` is the same 82574L. The driver serves the adapter over the packet
protocol (`witos/packet.h`), as the virtio network driver serves its device. The network service does not change: it
takes whichever adapter's packet channel the device manager hands it.

## 1. The pieces

| Where | What |
| --- | --- |
| `OutWit.OS.Hardware.IPacketDevice` and `PacketServer` | The packet protocol's server, moved out of the virtio network driver as H3.1 moved the block server: an attached window, `INFO`, a `SEND`'s frames checked whole before the adapter sees any, a `RECEIVE` answered from the adapter's frame queue or held until a frame comes. It serves any adapter that implements `IPacketDevice`: its address, link and MTU, its received frames, and its transmit. |
| `OutWit.OS.Hardware.E1000` (descriptors and addresses host-tested) | `E1000Descriptors`: the 82574's legacy receive and transmit descriptors of 16 bytes. `E1000Adapter`: the adapter as an `IPacketDevice` (§2). |
| `/system/Drivers/E1000` (`src/Services/Drivers/E1000`) | The driver: it takes the device, opens the adapter, logs its model, interrupt, address and link, and serves the packet protocol. Its stop resets the adapter, so that the adapter reads and writes no buffer once its pages go. |
| The device manager | `DriverTable` maps Intel's network controller 8086:10d3, the 82574L, to the driver. Ethernet controllers (PCI class 02/00) have no programming interface that tells them apart, so the vendor and the device name the driver. `DriverTable.ServesPackets` names the virtio adapter's driver and the e1000e's, whose packet channels the network probe and then the network service take. |
| WitOS.Dev | `BootRequest.NetworkAdapter`: virtio-net-pci, or QEMU's `e1000e`, in the same slot on the same INTx line. `runtime-devices` boots the PC's one disk (H3.3) with the e1000e (§4). |

## 2. The adapter

- **Taking it.** Whatever drove the adapter — the firmware's network driver may have left its rings running — the driver
  first masks its interrupts and stops its bus mastering (CTRL.GIO_MASTER_DISABLE, awaited until STATUS no longer reports
  a request pending). It then resets the adapter (CTRL.RST), which also reloads the NVM's words.
- **The address.** The driver reads the address the reset loaded into the receive address registers (RAL0 and RAH0 with
  AV). Where RAH0 marks none, it reads the NVM's words 0–2 through EERD. It writes the address back with AV set.
- **The filters.** The adapter accepts frames to its own address, broadcast frames (RCTL.BAM) and every multicast frame
  (RCTL.MPE). IPv6's neighbour discovery and router advertisements reach the host at its solicited-node and all-nodes
  groups, `33:33:…`, so every multicast frame comes in, as it does on the virtio device. The 128 multicast table entries
  are cleared.
- **The rings.** Legacy descriptors, which the adapter uses until RFCTL.EXTEN asks for extended receive descriptors:
  - 32 receive descriptors, each naming a 2048-byte buffer (RCTL.BSIZE). The adapter strips the frame's CRC (RCTL.SECRC).
    The driver takes a descriptor the adapter wrote back (DD) that is a whole frame (EOP) without a CRC, symbol,
    sequence or data error. It gives the descriptor back with the same buffer and moves RDT to the last descriptor it
    took.
  - 16 transmit descriptors, each a whole frame in a 2048-byte buffer. The adapter pads a short frame (TCTL.PSP) and adds
    the CRC (IFCS), and reports the descriptor done (RS). The ring holds 15 frames at a time, since a full ring's tail
    would meet its head. A `SEND` returns once the adapter took every frame. A transmit that sees no descriptor done in
    2 s fails that `SEND` with `EIO`, and the driver goes on serving.
  - The rings and the 48 buffers take 25 pages of one pinned arena.
- **The transmitter**: TCTL with the collision threshold and distance Intel's drivers set for full duplex, and TIPG's
  copper inter-packet gaps (8, 8, 6).
- **The link.** CTRL.SLU without forced speed or duplex, then up to 5 s for STATUS.LU. A link that stays down is
  reported, not refused; `INFO` reports the link as STATUS has it.
- **The interrupt.** An MSI message where the kernel serves messages and the function has the capability, the INTx line
  otherwise. The adapter interrupts for a transmit descriptor written back, a link change, the receive ring below half,
  a receive overrun and the receive timer. Outside MSI-X the adapter clears ICR when it is read. A service of the
  interrupt reads ICR, takes the received frames, frees the done transmit descriptors and unmasks the INTx line. A
  transmit's wait also looks at its descriptors every 10 ms, so that a lost interrupt delays it and no more.

## 3. Measured

**QEMU's e1000e** (8086:10d3, QEMU 11.1, `hw/net/e1000e_core.c`):

- MSI at configuration offset 0xD0 and MSI-X with 5 vectors in BAR 3.
- After a reset STATUS reports the link up at 1000 Mb/s full duplex. Autonegotiation runs only when the PHY is asked to
  restart it.
- The reset loads the permanent address into RAL0/RAH0 with AV: QEMU's first adapter, 52:54:00:12:34:56.
- Legacy receive descriptors unless RFCTL.EXTEN. ICR is cleared when it is read unless MSI-X is enabled.
- QEMU gives the adapter its option ROM, `efi-e1000e.rom` (iPXE), which the QEMU package carries; the firmware boots
  the disk, first in the boot order (`BdsDxe: starting Boot0001 "UEFI QEMU NVMe Ctrl witos-boot 1"`), with nothing of
  the ROM in the log.

**The gate.** `runtime-devices` boots the PC's one disk with an e1000e in place of the virtio adapter: from NVMe on the
three ISAs, then from AHCI on x64 and riscv64 (H3.3). The network service runs on the e1000e:

- the probe's ARP exchange with slirp's gateway;
- DHCP's lease;
- the DNS answer;
- the megabyte echoed through the runner's server, and the runner's 256 KiB through the port forward;
- `/bin/sockets-check`, IPv6 among it, whose link-local and global addresses come from slirp's router advertisement over
  the adapter's multicast;
- the stop, which resets the adapter.

| ISA | The PC's one disk from NVMe, on the e1000e | from AHCI, on the e1000e |
| --- | --- | --- |
| x64 (container) | passed: the adapter at 00:07.0 on MSI, the megabyte echoed in 953 ms of the guest's time | passed: on MSI, the megabyte in 1000 ms |
| ARM64 (container) | passed: the adapter at 00:06.0 on its INTx line, the megabyte in 1104 ms | — (the firmware has no SATA driver, H3.3) |
| riscv64 (container) | passed: the adapter at 00:06.0 on its INTx line, the megabyte in 1304 ms | passed: on its INTx line, the megabyte in 1298 ms |

The seven boots of the data disk before them passed again on the virtio adapter, through the packet server the e1000e
driver now shares; on x64 they echoed the megabyte in 850–866 ms. The e1000e copies each frame through its buffers, as
the virtio driver does, and reports its transmits one descriptor at a time.

## 4. What is not yet

- **VMware's adapter** is the H5 session's to measure: its e1000e (`ethernet0.virtualDev = "e1000e"` in the `.vmx`) is the
  same 82574L.
- **The e1000 (82540EM, 82545EM) and vmxnet3.** The e1000's registers and legacy descriptors are the e1000e's, less MSI
  and with its own NVM access; it would join the driver with a gate of its own. vmxnet3 waits for a need.
- **A real 82574.** Intel's drivers set the 82574's errata bits (TXDCTL, TARC, CTRL_EXT, GCR), which QEMU and VMware
  ignore. The driver sets none, since no real 82574 is a target.
- **Offloads, jumbo frames, VLANs and interrupt moderation**: checksums are computed by the stack, frames stay within
  1514 bytes, ITR stays 0.
