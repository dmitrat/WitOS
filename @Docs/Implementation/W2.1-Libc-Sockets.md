# W2.1 — the libc's sockets over the network service

Plan step W2.1, the third slice of `W0-Network-Inventory.md`. Processes reach the network service of W1.2: the libc
speaks the socket protocol (`witos/socket.h`) behind the Linux socket calls, so that musl's resolver and upstream .NET's
System.Native, built unchanged, see `AF_INET` sockets as they see them on Linux.

The step has two slices, each closing with its guest acceptance on x64, ARM64 and riscv64:

| Slice | What it adds | Guest acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **W2.1a** The libc's sockets | the service's endpoint in every process's start message; the socket calls, blocking and nonblocking, over one session per process; `poll` over sockets; `/etc/resolv.conf` from the lease and `/etc/hosts`; the network service on every board, the loopback alone without an adapter | a C program under the system layer: TCP to the runner's echo server and from the runner through the port forward, UDP and TCP over the loopback, a name through musl's resolver and slirp's forwarder, blocking calls, timeouts, `poll` |
| **W2.1b** .NET's sockets | sockets in `epoll`, which CoreCLR's socket engine waits on; the Network area of the compatibility suite (N1) | `Socket`, `TcpClient`, `TcpListener`, `UdpClient`, `NetworkStream` and `Dns` under CoreCLR, synchronous and asynchronous, in the guest as on the hosts |

## 1. The endpoint

A process reaches the network service as it reaches the namespace and the terminal services (D5.1, N4.3):

- **The service's answer.** The device manager always starts the network service: with the adapters its probes handed
  over, or with none. Without an adapter the stack serves the loopback alone, on a link that transmits nothing, so a
  process has sockets on every board, a board with a keyboard among them. The device manager names the service's
  clients' endpoint in its ready answer (`WIT_SERVICE_NETWORK`, `witos/manager.h`), after the namespace's and the
  terminal's, and the process manager keeps the first it receives.
- **The start message.** Every process the manager starts afterwards receives a duplicate with `SEND`
  (`WIT_START_NETWORK`, `witos/start.h`). It travels in the first message when there is room, otherwise in the second
  (`WIT_START_MORE`), and the libc keeps it as `WitProcessContext.Network`.

## 2. The libc's sockets

`src/Substrate/libc/socket.c` serves the Linux socket calls for `AF_INET`.

- **Calls.** It serves `socket`, `bind`, `connect`, `listen`, `accept` and `accept4`, `getsockname`, `getpeername`,
  `sendto`, `recvfrom`, `sendmsg`, `recvmsg`, `shutdown`, `getsockopt` and `setsockopt`. It also serves `read`, `readv`,
  `write` and `writev` on a socket's descriptor, and the `ioctl`s `FIONREAD` and `FIONBIO`. Plain `fcntl`, `dup`, `close`
  and `fstat` work as on Linux; `fstat` reports `S_IFSOCK`.
- **Refusals.** `AF_INET6` and `AF_UNIX` are `EAFNOSUPPORT`, and `socketpair` is refused, until W4. .NET then speaks
  IPv4 alone: its check for IPv6 creates an `AF_INET6` socket.
- **One session per process.** The session connects at the first socket and carries one request at a time, through the
  session's `Busy` word and a window of 16 pages. The wait for the reply holds no library lock.
- **Calls outside the table lock.** The socket calls run outside the table lock (`syscall.c`), as a pipe's transfers do.
  The lock is taken only to find a descriptor's socket and take a reference, or to install a new descriptor.
- **Records and references.** A socket's record (256 of them) holds its service identifier, its type, the timeouts and
  `SO_LINGER`. Its descriptors and the calls in flight reference it. After the last reference the service's `CLOSE` goes
  out once the table lock is free (`__wit_socket_flush`).
- **Waiting.** No request of the service waits.
  - A blocking call reads the poll word, asks, and on `EAGAIN` parks on the word. It wakes when the word moves, when
    `SO_RCVTIMEO` or `SO_SNDTIMEO` passes (`EAGAIN`, as Linux answers), or when a handler ran (`EINTR`), and then asks
    again.
  - A nonblocking call answers `EAGAIN`; `O_NONBLOCK` and `MSG_DONTWAIT` make a call nonblocking.
  - The network thread, a detached thread of the library's as the terminal's is, receives the service's readiness
    events. It counts each and bumps the poll word.
- **Connections.** A blocking `connect` of a stream waits until the socket is writable and returns its `SO_ERROR`. When
  `SO_SNDTIMEO` passes it returns `EINPROGRESS`, as Linux does.
- **Sending.**
  - A stream's `send` takes its bytes in pieces of the window, waiting for room, and returns what it took when the wait
    ends early.
  - `EPIPE` raises `SIGPIPE` unless `MSG_NOSIGNAL`.
  - A datagram goes whole or is `EMSGSIZE`.
- **Receiving.**
  - A stream's `recv` returns zero at the end of the stream and honours `MSG_WAITALL` and `MSG_PEEK`.
  - A datagram's `recv` cuts it to the room. `MSG_TRUNC` returns its whole length, and `recvmsg` reports the cut in
    `msg_flags`.
  - Ancillary data is not served: `sendmsg` with control bytes is `EOPNOTSUPP`, and `recvmsg` returns none.
- **Options.**
  - The library keeps `SO_RCVTIMEO`, `SO_SNDTIMEO` and `SO_LINGER`, and answers `SO_TYPE`, `SO_DOMAIN` and
    `SO_PROTOCOL` itself.
  - The service keeps `SO_REUSEADDR`, `SO_REUSEPORT` (W2.1b), `SO_BROADCAST`, `SO_SNDBUF`, `SO_RCVBUF`, `SO_KEEPALIVE`,
    `SO_ERROR`, `SO_ACCEPTCONN` and `TCP_NODELAY`.
  - `SO_REUSEPORT` came with W2.1b, since .NET's `ReuseAddress` sets it beside `SO_REUSEADDR` and reads it back. As on
    Linux, sockets that all set it share a port, listening ones too, and each sender's datagrams and connections go to one
    member of the group, chosen by the sender's address and port.
  - Every other option is `ENOPROTOOPT`.
- **poll.** `poll` asks the service for a socket's readiness outside the table lock, as it asks the terminal for a
  standard stream's, and parks on the poll word that the network thread bumps. `FIONREAD` is the bytes a read takes
  now; the service's `POLL` reply carries them in `Length`.
- **epoll (W2.1b).** CoreCLR's socket engine registers each socket edge-triggered with an epoll port and waits on the
  port from a thread of its own, as it does for a pipe (N4.2). `epoll.c` registers a socket by its record and its
  service identifier, without a reference, so that its last descriptor closes it at once, as on Linux; a look drops the
  entry once its descriptor no longer names that socket.
  - A wait takes three steps: under the table lock it reports the pipes' entries and gathers the sockets' questions,
    each with a reference to its record and its edge token (`__wit_epoll_probes`); outside the lock it asks the service
    for each socket's readiness (`__wit_socket_poll`); under the lock again it reports the sockets' entries
    (`__wit_epoll_report`) and lets the references go.
  - A socket's edge token is the count of every readiness event the process's session received, whatever its socket,
    read before the question, so that a change after the answer reports the entry again at the next wait. It cannot be
    the socket's own count: the service drops an event its full queue cannot take, since the events the queue holds wake
    the client, and a client asks again after any event (W1.2a). An entry whose state did not change is then reported
    once more, as Linux's epoll may, and its owner meets `EAGAIN`. W2.1b's first form took the socket's own count, and
    a riscv64 run of the suite with W2.2's checks hung in the megabyte's echo, then in every socket call after it. The
    service now reports at its stop how many events it could not deliver: 54, 141 and 5 in the suite's runs on x64,
    ARM64 and riscv64 with the fix, each of which passed, as did riscv64's two runs more (5 and 2).
  - With nothing to report, the wait parks as `poll` parks, on the word the network thread bumps.
  - `epoll_ctl` first drops every registration whose descriptor no longer names what it registered, as Linux drops a
    registration with its file's last descriptor. A closed socket's number names the next socket, which CoreCLR
    registers anew: a stale entry answered that `EPOLL_CTL_ADD` with `EEXIST`, or with `ENOSPC` once the 32 entries
    were full, and CoreCLR's socket engine turns a failed registration into an `InternalException` or an
    `OutOfMemoryException` of the operation. W2.2's HTTP checks met it: the suite's server stopped accepting at a
    `TcpListener` whose number a closed socket had held, and a POST waited for ever (W2.2).
  - An added or changed registration bumps the poll word, so that a wait parked meanwhile looks at it, as Linux's
    `epoll_ctl` wakes a waiter whose interest list now holds a ready descriptor: CoreCLR registers a socket after its
    first `EAGAIN`, when the readiness event of the bytes that end the wait may have woken the wait already.

## 3. Names

musl's resolver, built unchanged, reads `/etc/hosts` and `/etc/resolv.conf`, then asks the DNS server over the libc's
datagram sockets with `poll` and its own timeouts.

- **`/etc/hosts`.** The .NET install writes it with the loopback's names.
- **`/etc/resolv.conf`.** It is a text the libc makes, as `/etc/mtab` is (`files.c`'s `KindText`): a `nameserver` line
  for each DNS server of the lease.
  - It exists only in a process that has the service, and is read-only, a regular file to `stat`, and absent from
    directory listings.
  - The libc asks the service for the configuration before an open of the file, outside the table lock.

## 4. The acceptance

W2.1a's program is `tests/User/sockets_check.c`, `/bin/sockets-check` of the `runtime-devices` package. The scenario's
`/bin/init` starts it as a process like any other on every boot that is not cut off. The program checks:

- **Refusals.** `AF_INET6` and `AF_UNIX` are refused, and so is `socketpair`.
- **UDP over the loopback.**
  - binding the ephemeral port;
  - `sendto` and `recvfrom` with their addresses;
  - `poll`;
  - `FIONREAD`;
  - `MSG_PEEK` with `MSG_TRUNC`;
  - `MSG_DONTWAIT`'s `EAGAIN`.
- **TCP over the loopback.**
  - a nonblocking `connect`, then `poll` and `SO_ERROR`;
  - `accept4`'s address and `SOCK_CLOEXEC`;
  - `EAGAIN`, `read`, `write`, `FIONREAD` and `MSG_PEEK`;
  - `SO_RCVTIMEO` ending a blocking `recv`;
  - a blocking `send` of 512 KiB, more than both buffers, against a thread that reads it in order;
  - `shutdown` and the end of the stream;
  - `getpeername`;
  - a refused connection.
- **Names.**
  - `/etc/resolv.conf` names `10.0.2.3`;
  - `localhost` resolves through `/etc/hosts`;
  - `example.com` resolves through musl's resolver and slirp's forwarder.
- **The runner's echo server.** The program sends a quarter of a megabyte to `10.0.2.100:7` with blocking calls and
  checks every byte that comes back.
- **The runner's connection.** The program listens on port 8080 and echoes what the runner sends through the port
  forward. The runner checks its 256 KiB, as it checks the device manager's probe (W1.2b).

The scenario requires the program's lines and its exit with zero on the three ISAs.

W2.1b adds sockets to `epoll`, which CoreCLR's socket engine waits on. Its acceptance is `Socket`, `TcpClient`,
`TcpListener`, `UdpClient`, `NetworkStream` and `Dns` under CoreCLR, synchronous and asynchronous: the Network area of
the compatibility suite (N1) over the loopback on the hosts and in the guest.

## 5. What ran

W2.1a closed on 2026-10-19 with its acceptance on the three ISAs.

- **Host tests.** `OutWit.OS.Tests` passes 266 tests and `WitOS.Dev.Tests` 124; the format check is clean.
- **Guest.** `runtime-services` (in the Linux container, on the runtime tree an earlier `runtime-witos` built) passes on
  x64, ARM64 and riscv64, all five boots of `runtime-devices` on each. On each of the four boots that are not cut off,
  `/bin/sockets-check` passes its 47 checks and exits with 0: `example.com` resolves through musl's resolver and slirp's
  forwarder, the quarter megabyte comes back unchanged from the runner's echo server, and the runner's 256 KiB come back
  whole through the port forward from a connection the guest sees from `10.0.2.2`.
- **Without an adapter.** `runtime-terminal` and `runtime-compatibility` boot without a network adapter: the network
  service serves the loopback alone (`no adapter, serving sockets`), the device manager reports it ready with 0 adapters
  and stops it with the other services. Both pass on x64; the compatibility suite reports 89 checks, 2 recorded
  differences and 0 unexpected.
- **What W2.1a leaves to W2.1b.** Sockets in `epoll`, which CoreCLR's socket engine waits on, and the Network area of the
  compatibility suite. `getifaddrs`, which musl answers through netlink, stays with the interfaces of W4.

W2.1b closed on 2026-10-19 with its acceptance on the three ISAs, and with it step W2.1.

- **Host tests.** On the Linux host `OutWit.OS.Tests` passes 268 tests, among them `NetworkStackTests`' and `TcpTests`'
  new tests of `SO_REUSEPORT` (sockets that all set it share a port, a listening one too, a socket without it is refused,
  and the senders spread over the group); `WitOS.Dev.Tests` passes 122; the format check is clean. On the Windows host
  the compatibility suite reports 97 checks, 0 recorded differences, 0 unexpected, its 8 network checks passing.
- **Guest.** `runtime-services` and `runtime-compatibility` pass on x64, ARM64 and riscv64. The compatibility suite runs
  through `dotnet` on four processors and reports 97 checks, 3 recorded differences and 0 unexpected on each ISA: its
  Network area passes every check but `Network.IPv6`, the recorded difference — a megabyte echoed through `TcpListener`,
  `TcpClient` and `NetworkStream`, synchronous calls with a receive's timeout, the options (`NoDelay`, `ReuseAddress`, the
  buffers of 262144 bytes), `UdpClient`, a refused connection, the end of a stream and `localhost` through `Dns`.
- **What the first run found.** .NET's `ReuseAddress` sets `SO_REUSEPORT` before `SO_REUSEADDR` and reads it back
  (System.Native's `pal_networking.c`), which the service refused with `ENOPROTOOPT`; the stack now serves it as Linux's
  `bind` does (§2).
- **What W2.1 leaves.** IPv6 and Unix domain sockets (W4); `HttpClient` (W2.2); TLS (W3.1); `getifaddrs` and the
  interfaces (W4).
