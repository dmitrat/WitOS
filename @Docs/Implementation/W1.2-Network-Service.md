# W1.2 — the network service: a managed TCP/IP stack

Plan step W1.2, the second slice of `W0-Network-Inventory.md`. The network service `/system/Network` takes the network
driver's packet channel and runs IPv4 with ARP, ICMP, UDP, a DHCP client, loopback and TCP over it. It serves its
clients the socket protocol that the libc's sockets speak from W2.1. The stack is managed code, in the NativeAOT
service, as the author decided on 2026-10-19 (W0's decision 2).

The step has two slices, each closing with its guest acceptance on x64, ARM64 and riscv64:

| Slice | What it adds | Guest acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **W1.2a** The stack's base and datagrams | `OutWit.OS.Networking`: frames, ARP, IPv4, ICMP, UDP, the DHCP client, loopback; the service with the adapter the device manager hands it; the socket protocol's datagram half | the service's lease from QEMU's DHCP server; the device manager's probe asks slirp's forwarder for a name over a UDP socket of the service and gets an answer |
| **W1.2b** TCP | RFC 9293's state machine, retransmission by RFC 6298, NewReno (RFC 5681, 6582), MSS and window scaling, the socket protocol's stream half | the probe exchanges a megabyte with the runner's echo server through slirp and checks it; the runner connects to a listening socket through a port forward |

## 1. The stack

`OutWit.OS.Networking` is a library of the managed capability API (`src/OutWit.OS/Networking`) that only the network
service compiles in. It knows no channel and no device: it receives frames, transmits frames through an `ILink`, and
reads time from the caller. The host's tests therefore run two stacks against each other through a simulated link,
with loss and reordering when TCP comes.

- **Frames and packets.** Each header parses and writes itself, checked whole when it is read: Ethernet II, ARP,
  IPv4 (options skipped, fragments counted and dropped, so a sender sets don't-fragment), ICMP, UDP and later TCP. The
  Internet checksum is RFC 1071's.
- **ARP.** A cache of resolved neighbours. A packet to an unresolved one waits, a few packets per neighbour, while the
  request is retried; the cache answers requests for the stack's own address.
- **IPv4.** One interface with its address, mask, gateway and DNS servers, and the loopback interface for 127.0.0.0/8.
  A destination off the subnet goes to the gateway.
- **ICMP.** The stack answers echo requests, and reports port unreachable for a datagram to a port nobody bound.
- **UDP.** Sockets bound to a port and optionally connected to a peer, each with a bounded queue of datagrams.
- **DHCP.** RFC 2131's client — discover, offer, request, acknowledge — taking the address, the mask, the router and
  the DNS servers, and renewing at half the lease.
- **TCP** (W1.2b, `NetworkStack.Tcp.cs`, `TcpSocket`, `TcpHeader`, `ByteRing`).
  - **Connections.** RFC 9293's eleven states and its segment processing, sequence numbers compared modulo 2^32, random
    initial sequence numbers, RFC 5961's challenge acknowledgment for a reset or a SYN inside the window, a reset for a
    segment nobody takes.
  - **Options.** The MSS (the link's MTU less 40, 536 when the peer names none) and window scaling (RFC 7323): a
    connection's buffers are 256 KiB each, advertised with a shift of 3; a SYN's window is never scaled, and the right
    edge of an advertised window never moves back.
  - **Retransmission.** RFC 6298's timer with Karn's algorithm, from one second, between Linux's floor of 200 ms and a
    minute; Linux's six SYN retries and fifteen data retries before the connection fails with `ETIMEDOUT`.
  - **Congestion.** RFC 5681's slow start and congestion avoidance from an initial window of ten segments (RFC 6928),
    NewReno's fast retransmit and fast recovery on the third duplicate acknowledgment (RFC 6582), one segment after a
    timeout.
  - **Flow.** Delayed acknowledgments (every second full segment at once, otherwise within 40 ms), Nagle's algorithm
    unless `TCP_NODELAY`, the receiver's window update after reading, zero-window probes, out-of-order segments kept
    beyond a hole (64 segments, 256 KiB).
  - **Ends.** `SHUTDOWN` of either direction; a close sends its FIN after the bytes queued and lets the connection finish
    on its own, or resets it when bytes it received are unread, as Linux does; TIME-WAIT and an orphan's FIN-WAIT-2 last
    60 s, Linux's values.
  - **Listening.** A backlog of at most 128 connections, accepted or in SYN-RECEIVED; a SYN beyond it is dropped, and
    the peer's retransmission comes back.
  - **Ports.** TCP's own port space; sockets that all set `SO_REUSEADDR` share a port unless one of them listens, as
    Linux's bind decides.

## 2. The service

`/system/Network` (`src/Services/Network`) is a NativeAOT program that compiles `OutWit.OS.Networking` with the other
folders of the managed capability API.

- **Start.** The device manager probes each adapter (W1.1), starts the network service after the namespace service and
  moves each adapter's packet channel to it in a `WitNetworkAdapter` message (`witos/socket.h`), then one with
  `WIT_SOCKET_ADAPTERS_END` and no handle, as it moves disks to the namespace service. The service runs the stack on the
  first adapter. A second adapter is logged and left alone until a later step says how a second interface is configured.
- **The link.** `AdapterLink` is the stack's `ILink` over the packet channel and a window of 16 pages: frames arrive in the
  first half and leave from the second. The link keeps one `RECEIVE` outstanding and one `SEND` in flight. Frames the stack
  queues meanwhile wait, at most 512, and go packed in the next `SEND`. The driver answers a `SEND` while the `RECEIVE`
  waits, so the link tells the replies apart by their operation. The device manager's probe may leave a `RECEIVE` waiting
  when its wait ends; the service's `ATTACH` replaces the probe's window, and a reply to the probe's `RECEIVE` that comes
  first is passed over.
- **Threads.** One thread owns the packet channel's replies and the stack's timers. It waits on the packet channel, on an
  event the sessions set and until the stack's next deadline (`OBJECT_WAIT`), hands received frames to the stack, runs
  what is due and sends what the stack queued. Each client session has a thread of its own, as the namespace service's
  sessions do. A session calls the stack under the same lock, sends what the call queued, and sets the event only when the
  call moved the stack's next deadline before the one the thread waits for. libwitos gained `witos_event_set` for it
  (`Event.Set` in `OutWit.OS`).
- **Ready answer.** The service waits up to 20 seconds of the guest's time for the DHCP client's lease, then answers
  ready with its clients' endpoint (SEND, DUPLICATE, TRANSFER), with a lease or without one; without one the DHCP client
  goes on. The device manager connects to the service for its probe (§4). Processes receive the endpoint in their start
  message from W2.1, when the libc speaks the protocol.
- **Stop.** At the device manager's request (D6.1) every session finishes the request it is serving, closes its sockets
  and refuses later requests with `ESHUTDOWN`; the stack's thread stops and the service answers with its counters. The
  device manager stops the network service after the terminal and display services and before the namespace service and
  the drivers.

## 3. The socket protocol

`witos/socket.h`, with its codec in `OutWit.OS.Networking.SocketMessages` and a managed client,
`OutWit.OS.Networking.SocketClient`, which the device manager's probe uses.

- **Connecting.** A client connects once on the clients' endpoint with three handles:
  - the end of a session channel;
  - a window, a memory object of its own of at most 16 pages;
  - the end of an events channel.
- **Requests.** Requests go on the session one at a time, and every operation answers at once. An operation that would
  wait answers `EAGAIN`. The service then sends a readiness event (the socket and its bits: in, out, error, hang-up) on
  the events channel when that changes, and the libc builds blocking calls, `poll` and `epoll` on those events, as it
  builds them for pipes and the terminal. A full events queue drops the event: the events it holds wake the client
  already, and a client tries its request again after any event.
- **Ownership.** Every socket belongs to the session that opened it; another session's socket is `EBADF`, and a session's
  sockets close when its client closes the session's end.
- **Operations:**
  - `OPEN` (family, type, protocol), `BIND`, `CONNECT_TO` (`AF_UNSPEC` dissolves a datagram socket's association, as
    Linux's `connect` does), `LISTEN`, `ACCEPT`;
  - `SEND` and `RECEIVE` with an address for datagrams (`RECEIVE` reports the datagram's whole length, and `PEEK` leaves
    it queued);
  - `SHUTDOWN`, `CLOSE`;
  - `NAME` (local, or the peer's with `PEER`);
  - `POLL`;
  - `GET_OPTION` and `SET_OPTION` at `SOL_SOCKET`: `SO_TYPE`, `SO_ERROR`, `SO_BROADCAST`, `SO_REUSEADDR` (a port bound
    by sockets that all set it, as Linux allows; a datagram goes to one of them, a connected one first), and `SO_SNDBUF`
    and `SO_RCVBUF`, which report Linux's default and stay; any other option is `ENOPROTOOPT`;
  - `CONFIGURATION`: the interface's address, mask, router, DNS servers, MAC address and MTU, and whether DHCP leased
    them, from which the libc serves `/etc/resolv.conf` (W2.1). `INTERFACES`, for `getifaddrs`, comes with W2.1.
- **Data.** Data and addresses travel in the window and the request; a request's reserved words are zero.
- **Errors.** Errors are errno values.
- **Streams** (W1.2b). `OPEN` makes an `AF_INET` stream socket. `CONNECT_TO` answers `EINPROGRESS`, and the socket
  becomes writable once its connection is established, or reports its error through `SO_ERROR`, as a nonblocking
  connect does on Linux. `SEND` takes as many bytes as the send buffer holds; `RECEIVE` gives the bytes received in order,
  zero at the stream's end. `LISTEN` and `ACCEPT`; `SHUTDOWN` of `SHUT_RD`, `SHUT_WR` or `SHUT_RDWR`; `TCP_NODELAY` and
  `SO_ACCEPTCONN`. `SO_KEEPALIVE` reads zero, and setting it is `ENOPROTOOPT` until keep-alive probes are served. A
  socket's readiness event follows what its owner met: an empty receive buffer or a full send buffer, a `POLL` without
  the readiness asked, an accept with nothing to take.

## 4. The acceptance

The `runtime-devices` scenario boots with QEMU's user networking (W1.1). On each of its boots:

- the service reports the adapter, then the lease slirp's DHCP server gives — `10.0.2.15/24` from `10.0.2.2`, router
  `10.0.2.2`, DNS `10.0.2.3` — and that it serves sockets configured by DHCP;
- the device manager reports the service ready with one adapter, then its probe (`DnsProbe`): it connects to the service
  as a process will, reads the configuration, and asks the lease's DNS server, slirp's forwarder, for `example.com` over a
  datagram socket of the service. Any answer to the query proves the path — the socket, the stack's UDP, IPv4 and ARP,
  the driver and slirp — whatever the host's resolver makes of the name, so the scenario requires the answer and not its
  code. slirp forwards the query to the host's resolver, whose answer comes in the host's time while an idle guest's
  virtual time runs ahead under `-icount`'s `sleep=off`, so the probe tries twelve times, five seconds of the guest's
  time each;
- the service stops with the other services.

W1.2b adds TCP to every boot (`TcpProbe`), with the runner's side of the guest's network (`NetworkPeer`):

- **Outbound.** The runner starts an echo server on the host's loopback before QEMU, and QEMU's user networking forwards
  `10.0.2.100:7` to it (`guestfwd`, `BootRequest.NetworkEcho`), whatever port the server got. The probe connects there
  through the service and exchanges a megabyte with it, checking every byte that comes back.
- **Inbound.** The probe listens on port 8080 and says so; QEMU forwards a free port of the host's loopback to it
  (`hostfwd`, `BootRequest.NetworkForward`). On that line the runner connects (`BootActionKind.Connect`), sends 256 KiB,
  ends its sending and requires the same bytes back before the probe ends its own; the probe reports what it echoed and
  the peer slirp shows it, `10.0.2.2`. A connection that fails or bytes that do not come back whole end the boot as a
  failed action.

## 5. What ran

W1.2a closed on 2026-10-19 with its acceptance on the three ISAs.

- **Host tests.** `OutWit.OS.Tests` passes 255 tests, among them the eight of `NetworkStackTests` (the checksum of RFC 1071,
  the headers, ARP between two stacks and its retries, UDP over the segment and the loopback, bindings, ICMP's echo and
  port unreachable, the DHCP lease and its retransmissions) and the three of `SocketMessagesTests`; `WitOS.Dev.Tests`
  passes 121; the format check is clean.
- **Guest.** `runtime-services` (in the Linux container, on the runtime tree an earlier `runtime-witos` built) passes on
  x64, ARM64 and riscv64, all five boots of `runtime-devices` on each. On every boot the service leases `10.0.2.15/24`
  from slirp for 86400 s, the device manager's probe reads the configuration through the service, and slirp's forwarder
  answers the query for `example.com` on the first attempt (`rcode 0, 2 answers`). The service stops with 0 sessions and
  its counters: on x64's first boot 4 frames received and 4 sent.
- **What W1.2a leaves to W1.2b.** TCP and the protocol's stream half; processes reach the service from W2.1, when the
  libc speaks the protocol and the process manager passes the endpoint in the start message.

W1.2b closed on 2026-10-19 with its acceptance on the three ISAs.

- **Host tests.** `OutWit.OS.Tests` passes 266 tests, among them the eleven of `TcpTests`: the handshake, data both ways
  and the close through TIME-WAIT; a megabyte echoed whole over a clean segment with no retransmission, and over one
  that loses 2% and swaps 5% of its frames, where retransmissions and fast retransmits recover every loss; a refusal
  reported once through `SO_ERROR`; a silent peer that times out after the SYN's retries; a closed window, probed until
  the reader drains it; a backlog of one, whose second request comes back with the SYN's retransmission; the reset of a
  close with unread bytes and `EPIPE` after it; a simultaneous close; a connection over the loopback; the header's
  options. `WitOS.Dev.Tests` passes 121; the format check is clean.
- **Guest.** `runtime-services` passes on x64, ARM64 and riscv64, all five boots of `runtime-devices` on each. On every
  boot the probe's megabyte comes back unchanged from the runner's echo server (on the first boot in 858 ms of the guest's
  time on x64, 994 ms on ARM64 and 1135 ms on riscv64), and the runner's 256 KiB come back whole through the port
  forward from a connection the guest sees from `10.0.2.2`. The service's counters after x64's first boot: 1859 frames
  received and 1410 sent.
- **What W1.2b leaves.** Keep-alive probes, urgent data and the SACK, timestamp and window-probe refinements beyond
  RFC 9293's core; IPv6. Processes reach the service from W2.1.
