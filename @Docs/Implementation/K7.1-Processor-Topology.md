# K7.1 — Processor topology (ABI v63)

Plan step K7.1 is the descriptive half of the SMP groundwork of [RFC 0011 v3 §7.9](../RFC-0011-Kernel-Architecture-and-ABI.md):
the kernel learns the processors of the board from the platform once at boot, `PROCESSOR_QUERY` (93) reports them in
a record form beside the 4-byte form of the frozen line, and `THREAD_AFFINITY` (41) with the right `AFFINITY`
(1048576) records where a thread may run. Nothing runs on a second processor yet: K7.2 brings the per-processor state,
the start of secondary processors, inter-processor interrupts, the remote fence of `PROCESS_WRITE_BARRIER` and TLB
shootdown. The call-by-call reference is [ABI-Reference.md](ABI-Reference.md).

## What changed

**The platform enumerates processors.** `wit_platform_processors(boot, table, capacity)` fills
`WitProcessorDescriptor` records (hardware identity, usable or not) in the firmware's order. On q35 the RSDP the loader
published leads to the XSDT (or a revision 1 RSDT), its entries to the MADT, and the MADT's Processor Local APIC and
Local x2APIC entries name the processors, usable when enabled or online capable; every table's signature, length and
checksum are verified before its body is read, pages of reserved memory are mapped once as device pages and pages of
usable RAM are read through the identity map; nothing else of ACPI is interpreted. On virt the children of `/cpus`
whose `device_type` is `cpu` give their `reg` (the MPIDR affinity in one or two cells, as `#address-cells` says) and
their `status`.

**The kernel keeps a table.** `processors.c` validates the platform's list against the running processor's identity
(`wit_arch_processor_id`: the x2APIC id of CPUID leaf 0xB or the initial APIC id of leaf 1; the MPIDR affinity fields)
— a table that does not name the boot processor is a panic, a repeated identity too — and numbers the boot processor
first, then the usable others in the firmware's order, up to `WIT_PROCESSOR_CAPACITY` (8). The boot processor is
`ONLINE | BOOT`; the others are present and offline. The boot log says `Processors present/online: N/1`. The old
compile-time constant of one processor is gone: the callers that need the single-processor invariant (the write
barrier, the cache size, the suspend and context calls) ask `wit_processors_online()`, which is 1 until K7.2.

**`PROCESSOR_QUERY` in two forms.** With size 4 it is the frozen line's: the current processor as `{group:u16,
number:u8, reserved:u8}`. With size 280 and the caller's `Version` 1 and `Size` it copies `WitProcessorInfo`: the index
of the current processor, the processors present and online, and one `WitProcessorRecord` per processor — hardware
identity, flags, group and number, the largest cache (`wit_arch_cache_size`) and the ISA feature words of an online
processor (`wit_arch_processor_features`: CPUID.1 ECX and EDX on x64; ID_AA64ISAR0_EL1 and ID_AA64PFR0_EL1 low words
on ARM64), zero for an offline one. A wrong size is `INVALID_ARGUMENT`, a foreign version `UNSUPPORTED`; the whole
destination is validated first. `CPU_CACHE_SIZE` (208) stays with the frozen line until K8.

**`THREAD_AFFINITY(thread handle or WIT_THREAD_SELF, mask pointer, 0 get / 1 set)`.** A thread's mask starts as the
boot processor's bit. Reading needs `QUERY`, setting the new right `AFFINITY`, which every thread handle carries
(`WIT_RIGHT_THREAD_ALL` grows to 1055220). A set mask must be nonzero and within the processors present
(`INVALID_ARGUMENT`) and name an online processor (`UNSUPPORTED` otherwise): placement elsewhere than the boot processor
arrives with phase P. A thread of another process is read and waited for, not driven, as before.

**Scenarios with two processors.** `BootRequest.Processors` reaches QEMU's `-smp`; `boot-smp2` on both boards boots
the self-test kernel with two processors and requires the line `Processors present/online: 2/1` besides every marker;
the firmware parks the second processor, which the kernel leaves alone until K7.2.

## Kernel

- `processor.h` (ABI: `WitProcessorInfo`, `WitProcessorRecord`, the flags, the affinity flags; kernel-internal:
  `WitProcessorDescriptor`, the table's interface); `limits.h`: `WIT_PROCESSOR_CAPACITY`; `user_abi.h`: v63, call 41,
  the `AFFINITY` right, the two forms of 93; `user_layout.h`: the constant removed.
- `processors.c`; `kernel.c`: the table after the clocks. `Kernel.Platform.Q35/acpi.c`; `Kernel.Platform.QemuVirt/devices.c`:
  `wit_platform_processors`. `Kernel.Arch.X64/processor.c`; `Kernel.Arch.A64/processor.c`, `processor.asm`.
- `user_calls.c`: `processor_query` in both forms, `thread_affinity`, the online count; `user_thread.c`: the default
  mask, `wit_user_thread_affinity`; `user.h`: `Affinity` on the thread.
- `tools/WitOS.Dev`: `BootRequest.Processors`, `RequiredLines`, the `boot-smp2` scenarios of both suites.

## Tests

`tests/Kernel/processor_tests.c` (`Processors.Topology`, both foundations, any processor count): the table has one to
eight processors, the boot processor alone is online and current, its record carries its identity, flags, features
and cache, every other record is present and offline with a distinct identity, nothing lies beyond the table, and the
affinity rule accepts the boot processor, refuses an empty mask and a mask beyond the table and, with two processors,
reports a mask of the offline one unsupported. `tests/User.X64/processors.asm` (over `SYSCALL`),
`tests/User.A64/processors.asm` and `tests/Kernel/user_processor_tests.c` (`User.ProcessorsAndAffinity`): both forms of
`PROCESSOR_QUERY`, a wrong size and a foreign version refused, the default affinity, a set of the boot processor, an
empty mask and a mask beyond the table refused, a foreign flag refused, and a duplicate with `QUERY` alone reading
the mask but refused the set. `boot-smp2` passes on both boards with `Processors present/online: 2/1`.

## Limits kept explicit

- Descriptive only: no processor but the boot one runs anything; `Online` is 1, `Current` is 0, and affinity is a
  record the scheduler of phase P will honour.
- The table holds eight processors; the firmware's further processors are not listed.
- Features and cache sizes are reported for online processors alone; an offline processor's words are zero until it
  starts (K7.2).
- The virt processors come from the device tree, the q35 ones from the MADT; a board with neither reports the boot
  processor alone only if its platform says so — the kernel refuses a table without the boot processor.
