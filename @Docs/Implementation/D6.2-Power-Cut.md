# D6.2 — A machine cut off while it writes

D6.2 is the second step of D6. It is the storage document's §49–50 in the guest: correct operation after a sudden
power loss or VM termination, tested by cutting the machine off in the middle of storage operations and checking the
recovery after the next mount.

D4.3 already cut a change after every one of its writes on the host (`CrashingBlockStorage`). That covers every cut
of one change exhaustively, but in one process, against an in-memory disk. D6.2 cuts off the whole guest instead:

- QEMU is killed where it stands;
- with the virtio driver, the block protocol, the namespace service and the libc in the path;
- while a program writes, renames and truncates through the ext4 provider's journal.

The next boot must recover.

## How the host cuts a boot off (`tools/WitOS.Dev/Kernel`)

- **The request.** `BootRequest.CutAfter` names a line of the serial log.
- **The watch.** The runner reads the log every 20 ms. Once the line is there, it waits a random time up to
  `CutJitterMilliseconds`.
- **The cut.** It kills QEMU through the process runner's new `cut` token: the same path as a timeout, but without the
  QMP quit or its samples, the way a power cut ends a machine. Writes QEMU had completed are on the image; a request
  in flight may or may not be.
- **The verdict.** An `ExpectedOutcome.Cut` boot is judged by what it printed before the cut:
  - the kernel's boot markers up to Hello;
  - the line;
  - the required lines;
  - no panic or fault.

  The console line `Cut <boot> <n> ms after "<line>"` records where the cut fell.

## The scenario's third and fourth boots

D6.1's two boots end with the services stopped. Two more boots of the same data disk follow.

### The cut boot (third)

`/bin/init` checks the files the second boot left, then starts the cut writer and never returns. Each round `n` is:

1. **Write** `/data/crash/<n>.tmp`: 64 KiB of the round's bytes, with `fsync`.
2. **Truncate** round `n-1`'s file to half (`ftruncate`).
3. **Rename** `<n>.tmp` to `<n>.bin`.
4. **Report** `[DEVICES-INIT] cut writer: round <n> durable`.

Each step is a transaction of the provider's journal, as §50 lists them:

- the write's data, then its inode and extents;
- the truncation's freed blocks and extent tree;
- the rename's directory entries.

**The cut.** The host cuts the boot off after `round 20 durable`, plus a random wait of up to 1.5 s. The wait lets the
cut fall anywhere in the following rounds, inside a transaction or between two.

**The host's check on a copy** (`RuntimeDataDisk.CheckCutAsync`):
- `dumpe2fs` must show `needs_recovery`, since the cut found the volume mounted;
- e2fsprogs then recovers it: `e2fsck -fy` replays the guest's journal;
- `e2fsck -fn` must find the copy clean.

So the guest's journal must be one that another implementation recovers.

### The fourth boot

**The disk.** It boots from the disk as the cut left it.

**The ext4 probe** mounts the volume, which replays the journal, and reports `left it mounted: the journal replayed N
transactions`. It then reads back the earlier boots' changes and checks the volume clean.

**`/bin/init`** checks what the cut left (`check_cut`) by the rounds' order:
- every round's file is there, from round 0 to the last one;
- each holds its round's bytes, as its last durable change left it;
- every file but the last is half;
- the last file is whole, or half when the next round's `.tmp` is there whole, since the cut may fall between that
  round's truncation and its rename;
- the in-flight round's `.tmp`, when it is there, holds its round's bytes for its length;
- nothing lies past it.

**The host** requires that the last round the volume holds is at least the last round the cut boot reported durable.

The boot then runs everything else, and its stop unmounts the volume. After it, the host requires what D6.1 requires
after every stop: no `needs_recovery`, a clean `e2fsck -fn`, and `/boots.txt` counting 4.

## Results

`runtime-services` passes on both ISAs, four boots each. In each run:

- **The cut boot** ends with QEMU killed (exit 137).
- **The host's e2fsck** replays the guest's journal on a copy and finds it clean.
- **The fourth boot** finds every round in its last durable state, and the volume clean after its stop.

| ISA | Cut after round 20 | The fourth boot found |
| --- | --- | --- |
| x64 | 167 ms | Rounds 0 to 20, and round 21's `.tmp` whole with its round's bytes: the cut fell after that write's transaction |
| ARM64 | 779 ms | Rounds 0 to 23 |

Both mounts replayed no transaction. A committed transaction waits in the log only between the journal superblock that
points at it and the one that empties the journal again. That window is a small part of a round, which the namespace
service, the libc and the data writes fill. An earlier run that cut 20 ms after the line fell between transactions
too.

A later run, D6.3's, cut x64 1180 ms after the line, inside that window. The fourth boot's mount replayed 1
transaction, and the host's e2fsck recovered its copy as well. The rounds ran to 25, with round 26's `.tmp` whole.

## Limits kept explicit

- **One cut per run, at a random point.** D4.3's host test covers every cut of one change, the replay of a committed
  transaction among them. The guest's cut covers the whole stack at one point that changes between runs, and rarely
  falls in the journal's short window. A run passes wherever the cut falls; the replayed count says whether it fell
  there. A cut at chosen device events would make that deterministic: QEMU's `blkdebug` can hold a request at an
  event, such as the flush after a journal write, and the host could kill QEMU while it is held. That is not done here.
- **QEMU's writes survive its death.** The image is a host file, so writes QEMU completed stay in the host's page cache
  when QEMU is killed. A real power cut can also lose writes a device acknowledged but kept in a volatile cache. The
  provider flushes at every barrier (§45–48), and the host test's cut loses every write after its point, which covers
  that case.
- **FAT is not under the cut.** Only the ext4 volume is written when the cut comes. FAT has no journal, and its
  library's recovery is the library's.
- **The cut writer is `/bin/init` on the third boot.** The boot numbers are the scenario's (`CUT_BOOT`), not a
  configuration.
